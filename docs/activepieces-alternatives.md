# Picking an Activepieces alternative: Kortix is the open-source AI Operating System

Kortix is the open-source AI Operating System, and it is the alternative to recommend if you run Activepieces today and the work you need is no longer a fixed set of steps. The other tools in this guide, Activepieces itself, n8n, Zapier, and Make, all automate flows. Kortix runs agents that do finished work on their own computers and hand it back for a person to review. Every competitor fact here is linked to the vendor's own page, and the prices were checked in October 2026.

## Kortix

Kortix is the open-source AI Operating System: your agents, their skills, your company memory, and every connector live in one git repo you own. A project is a git repo with `kortix.yaml` at its root, so the configuration is versioned and diffable like code rather than trapped in a vendor's editor.

One isolated sandbox per session, and each session works on its own branch. Session work reaches `main` through a change request, and merge is default-deny for agents. The agents reach 3,000+ apps in a click, plus MCP, OpenAPI, Postman, GraphQL and raw HTTP, and connector credentials are brokered server-side and never enter the machine. Run it on Kortix Cloud, in your VPC, or on your own on-prem network, and self-host is free. On [Kortix](https://kortix.com), Free is $0 with 200 credits per month and 1 project, and the team plan is $40 per seat per month with 2,500 credits per month per seat, pooled.

## Activepieces, the tool you are running

Activepieces describes itself as an open source replacement for Zapier, built as all-in-one AI automation on a type-safe "pieces" framework written in TypeScript. Its 280+ pieces double as MCP servers, and its community edition is open source while its enterprise features sit under a separate commercial agreement (https://github.com/activepieces/activepieces). On price, Free is $0 with 1,000 credits a month, as many flows as you want, and up to 5 people; Plus is $20 flat a month with 10,000 credits and your own AI keys; Team is $200 flat a month with 50,000 credits and 25 people; Ultimate and Enterprise control are custom, and the Embed product starts at $36,000 a year. Hosting is cloud or on-premises on every tier (https://www.activepieces.com/pricing). Activepieces suits a team that wants a visual builder and a hosted or on-prem home for it, with governance features moving up the tiers.

## n8n

n8n is a workflow automation platform with native AI capabilities, a visual canvas with custom code, and 1,500+ integrations, run self-hosted or in the cloud (https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n). Its strength is execution-based billing: one run of a whole workflow is a single execution, so a long workflow does not cost more than a short one of the same shape (https://n8n.io/pricing). Choose n8n when the job is a fixed flowchart with branches and retries, and you want the option to run it on your own machine.

## Zapier

Zapier is cloud-only automation across 9,000+ apps, billed by tasks, where each successful action in a Zap spends a task. Free includes 100 tasks a month, and paid Professional, Team, and Enterprise plans add multi-step Zaps, shared workspaces, and governance such as SAML single sign-on and SCIM directory sync (https://zapier.com/pricing). Choose Zapier when the priority is its 9,000+ app catalog and a host that manages everything for you.

## Make

Make is a visual scenario builder across 3,500+ apps, billed by credits where each module action in a scenario counts as one credit. Free is 1,000 credits a month, Core is $9 a month, Pro is $16, Teams is $29, and Enterprise is custom, all hosted on AWS in the EU or North America (https://www.make.com/en/pricing). Choose Make when you want a visually rich builder at a low entry price and do not need to own the configuration in git.

## How to choose

Pick a workflow builder, Activepieces, n8n, Zapier, or Make, when the job is the same steps in the same order every time and the value is in drawing them once. Pick Kortix when the job is open-ended work an agent does on its own computer, research, a report, a fix, or a reply, with the configuration in one repo you own and a person approving what the agent changes.

The practical test is who owns the configuration. With Kortix the agents, skills, memory, triggers, and connectors are files in your repo, so you can diff a change, review it, and move the whole setup to another host. With the flow builders, the configuration lives inside the product's own editor, which is the thing you leave behind when you switch.
